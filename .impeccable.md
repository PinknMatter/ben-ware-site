# Design Context

## Users
Visitors to Ben Ware's personal portfolio: editors, journalists, event organisers,
academic peers, and readers who just finished one of his books. They arrive to find
out who he is, what he's written, and how to reach or book him. Browsed at leisure,
mostly desktop, editorial mindset.

## Brand Personality
Literary, incisive, contemporary. A philosopher of ends and beginnings — the site
should feel like something a good creative agency would build for an author:
curated, confident, quiet. White space does the work; type is the interface.

## Aesthetic Direction
- Pure white page, ink-only palette; the provisional red (`--color-accent`) appears
  ONLY as interaction feedback (hover/focus).
- V1 hero (fixed, keep as-is): Space Mono identity, book covers in grey mats,
  giant "BenWare" wordmark.
- Below the hero: editorial catalogue. Space Mono for eyebrows/meta; Spectral
  (light weights) for statements, titles and pull-quotes. Grey `#e9e9e7` mats and
  the oversized quotation glyph tie back to the hero.
- One left edge, numbered sections ("01 — Writing"), hairline rules, generous and
  varied vertical rhythm. Disciplined asymmetry, never random floating.
- Anti-references: no cards-in-cards, no gradients, no centred-everything, no
  icon-above-heading templates.

## Design Principles
1. Text-based by design: no dependency on imagery — type, rules and white space carry the page.
2. The hero's language (mats, mono eyebrows, index numerals) continues below the fold, translated to an editorial register.
3. Red is rare: hover-only, so interaction feels like annotation.
4. Ben's own voice leads: statement and verbatim quotes outscale the lists.
5. Every section shares one alignment system; unevenness is fixed by rhythm, not decoration.
